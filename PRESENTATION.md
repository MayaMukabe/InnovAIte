# Brain Builder Hackathon Presentation

## One-sentence pitch

Brain Builder turns independent thinking into a city students can grow—and turns AI from an answer machine into a coach.

## 2:30 presentation script

### 0:00–0:20 — Hook

Meet Maya, a 14-year-old who uses AI whenever homework gets difficult. She finishes faster, but she is practicing how to ask for answers—not how to reason through uncertainty. What if every independent thought helped her build something she was proud of?

**Show:** Brain City and its five cognitive districts.

### 0:21–0:40 — Problem

College Board found that high-school students using generative AI for schoolwork increased from 79% to 84% between January and May 2025. AI access is not the enemy. Passive use is. Students still need productive struggle, useful feedback, and confidence in their own thinking.

**Do:** Open the Logic District quest.

### 0:41–1:30 — Demo

Brain Builder represents Maya’s mind as a city. Each district represents a cognitive skill, and completing learning experiences upgrades its buildings.

Before Maya receives help, she must pause, create a strategy, and attempt the problem.

**Do:** Submit the rehearsed incorrect answer.

She does not receive the solution. Spark, her AI coach, provides a process hint and gives control back to her.

**Do:** Submit the correct answer, write the strategy explanation, and show the district upgrade.

Her effort becomes visible growth—not merely another quiz score. The Boss Battle then checks whether she can transfer that learning independently, under pressure, without hints.

**Do:** Open the prepared Boss Battle and land one correct attack.

A correct strategy causes animated damage, builds a combo, and moves Maya closer to defeating the Glitch.

### 1:31–1:50 — Human–AI collaboration

The student controls the strategy, answer, hint timing, explanation, and retry. AI may coach the process or transform approved notes into grounded practice, but it never owns mathematical truth. Correctness remains in a reviewed, server-validated question bank.

### 1:51–2:15 — Impact and feasibility

We would pilot Brain Builder with middle- and high-school learners. We would measure no-hint completion, explanation quality, delayed transfer, persistence, and confidence—not screen time.

The prototype already demonstrates cognitive districts, learning quests, grounded material assessment, progress persistence, and real-time Boss Battles. Secure school accounts, educator review tools, managed storage, accessibility testing, and student-data protections are our next production steps.

### 2:16–2:30 — Close

In an AI-first world, the winning skill is not getting answers faster. It is building a mind strong enough to know what to do with them. Help us make every student the hero of their own thinking.

## Demo controls

## Mentor questions

### What problem are you solving?

Students who use AI for instant answers can lose the productive struggle needed to build reasoning, confidence, and learner agency.

### What is the prototype proving?

It proves that an AI-powered experience can require thinking before guidance, keep correctness outside the language model, reward explanation, and make cognitive growth visible.

### What remains under human control?

Students control their strategy, attempts, hints, explanations, retries, uploaded material, and progress exports. Educators should control curriculum approval and review.

### What can AI do?

AI can provide process coaching, adapt guidance, and turn approved materials into source-grounded practice. It cannot silently decide correctness or learner ability.

### What are the major risks?

- **Accuracy:** Coaching can be wrong, so reviewed content owns correctness.
- **Bias:** Explanation feedback must support varied ages, dialects, and English fluency.
- **Privacy:** Real deployment requires data minimization, consent, deletion, and secure storage.
- **Safety:** Feedback must encourage students without ridicule or shame.
- **Overreliance:** Guidance appears after an attempt, and Boss Battles provide no hints.

## Likely judge questions

### How is this different from a quiz app?

Brain Builder rewards attempts, revision, explanation, persistence, and transfer. Those behaviors create persistent visual growth rather than simply producing a score.

### Why is AI necessary?

AI can adapt coaching and rapidly transform a learner’s approved notes into practice. Its role is deliberately constrained so it supports rather than replaces thinking.

### How will you measure success?

We will measure independent completion, explanation quality, delayed transfer, persistence after mistakes, confidence, and reduced answer reveals.

### Is this ready for student data?

Not yet. This is a functional hackathon prototype. Authentication, managed storage, educator and guardian controls, security review, and COPPA/FERPA implementation are release requirements.
